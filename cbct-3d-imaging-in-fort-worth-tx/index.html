What it is

CBCT 3D imaging at Polished Family Dental in Fort Worth

CBCT stands for cone beam computed tomography. Regular dental X-rays are flat, two-dimensional pictures. A CBCT scan takes a series of images as the scanner turns around your head and combines them into a 3D model of your teeth, roots, nerves, sinuses and jawbone.

That 3D view lets our dentists measure bone, see where nerves run and spot problems a flat X-ray can miss, so treatment is planned with more accuracy and fewer surprises. At our Fort Worth office on 7540 S Hulen St, we use 3D imaging when a closer look will help your care, for patients from Southwest Fort Worth, Wedgwood, Hulen, Candleridge and nearby.

When we use it

  • Planning dental implants
  • Complex root canals and hard-to-find infections
  • Wisdom teeth and impacted teeth near nerves
  • Checking the jaw joints, sinuses and airway

Questions

Common questions

Is a CBCT scan safe?

Yes. A dental CBCT scan uses a low dose of radiation, much lower than a medical CT scan. We only recommend one when the 3D view will help your diagnosis or treatment.

Does it hurt?

No. There are no needles or numbing. You just hold still while the scanner turns around your head, usually for less than a minute.

How is CBCT different from regular X-rays?

Regular dental X-rays show a flat, 2D picture. CBCT shows your teeth, bone and nerves in 3D, so we can see depth, angles and exact measurements.
